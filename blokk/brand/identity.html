The one that holds things back.

The mark is the journal, drawn. An append-only stack of slabs seen isometrically — each one a completed step. The top slab floats: that's the step that's queued and waiting on you, and the exposed blue face is the slot the next one drops into. At 16px it's a cube. At full size it's a stack that isn't finished yet, which is the honest state of the system almost all the time.

The O is the block's top face — same rhombus, same blue, same 45° at 0.62 vertical scale. It's the one detail that ties the wordmark to the mark, and it means the name can be set without the cube beside it and still be recognisably Blokk.

Ask

The chat mark is not a speech bubble with the logo on it. The body is the block's top face — the same rhombus as the mark and the wordmark's O, the same 45° at 0.568, the same blue — because that face is already "the slot the next one drops into", and an ask box is that slot. The tail's two upper corners sit on the rhombus's lower-left edge and its apex continues that edge, so it belongs to the geometry rather than being stuck underneath; drawn straight down it read as a map pin. A third slab below it, the queued one, was tried and cut — at 20px it read as a speck of dirt on the screen. Generated by brand/chatmark.py, which centres it on its own bounds: the body's centre is not the composition's centre once a tail hangs off one corner.
